 # Manuscript Plan: Ploidy-Dependent Glucose-Starvation Ecology

  ## Summary

  - This plan was steered primarily by user_interpretation_31_03_26.txt and narrative_reframing_package.txt;
    implementers may consult them for context but should not reinterpret them into a new thesis.
  - Central manuscript claim: high ploidy changes glucose-starvation ecology in ways that affect growth,
    death, glucose use, and predicted selection under resource scarcity.
  - The manuscript should not be organized around predictive transfer, SUM-159-fuse exclusion, or a volume-
    first mechanism. Those are supporting analyses, limitations, or mechanistic refinements.

  ## Narrative Spine

  - Result 1: Establish the experimental system and phenotype. Show live/dead-cell and glucose trajectories
    across cell lines, ploidy states, and starting glucose. Focus on ploidy-dependent starvation responses
    rather than on surprise as the main rhetorical device.
  - Result 2: Show why richer consumer-resource models are needed. Present the glucose-only model as an
    insufficient baseline, then motivate hidden-resource, pathway, or waste structure using observed
    trajectory mismatches.
  - Result 3: Extract the shared ploidy-effect interpretation. Summarize model-inferred ploidy effects on
    apparent glucose uptake/depletion, yield, and starvation-associated maintenance/death. Use guarded
    language: these are fitted apparent parameters, not direct metabolic-flux or ATP measurements.
  - Result 4: Convert the fitted tradeoff into ecological prediction. Present selection simulations showing
    conditions where scarcity favors high ploidy and resource-replete conditions may favor low ploidy. Frame
    this as the main modeling payoff.
  - Result 5: Define generalization limits. Predictive transfer is a stress test. Failed or mixed transfer
    means the learned effect is not a universal held-out-line predictor; it does not become the manuscript’s
    verdict.
  - Result 6: Explain heterogeneity. SUM-159-fuse, morphology/volume, nuclear-size results, protocol
    differences, and possible cell-state effects should be used to explain why the ploidy effect is context-
    dependent.

  ## Handling Conflicting Existing Framings

  - The user interpretation and narrative reframing take precedence over repo artifacts when deciding
    narrative emphasis.
  - Do not use WP1-6 as the scientific spine. Reuse useful figures, audit tables, and extended analyses, but
    treat the WP1-6 framing as conceptually secondary.
  - Do not let transfer benchmarks define success or failure. They belong in robustness/limitations.
  - Do not promote the volume/SUM-159-fuse story into the central claim. Current evidence supports morphology/
    volume as an important axis of heterogeneity, not as a complete explanation.
  - Do not make SUM-159-fuse exclusion a rescue strategy. Treat it as an informative exception with
    biological, protocol, and morphology context.

  ## Figure And Evidence Plan

  - Figure 1: Experimental design and representative live/dead/glucose trajectories.
  - Figure 2: Model-free ploidy-response summaries visible directly in the data.
  - Figure 3: Consumer-resource model progression and representative model fit overlays.
  - Figure 4: Shared ploidy-effect parameter summary across model families.
  - Figure 5: Selection landscape showing predicted regimes favoring high versus low ploidy.
  - Figure 6 or Supplement: Heterogeneity and limits: transfer, SUM-159-fuse, morphology/volume, nuclear size,
    and competition checks.
