Publication work packages for a first draft

Draft-level thesis

  Ploidy changes glucose-starvation physiology primarily through cell-size /
  resource-demand-linked effects, but these effects are not universal across all
  engineered comparisons. SUM-159-fuse is a biologically informative exception
  because it is the only cell-line dataset built from two batches with differing
  protocol details, lacks the expected volume separation, and weakens or dilutes
  the transferable mechanistic conclusion: the ploidy-effect directions on y1,
  v1, K1, and m are conserved across tested models but move further from zero
  and show stronger inter-model agreement after SUM-159-fuse is excluded. The
  first draft should lead with
  observed trajectories,
  model-free ploidy effects, and morphology/volume; mechanistic ODE claims
  should be promoted only after a visually credible representative model is
  selected.

WP1. Establish core starvation phenotype

  Question
    Do high-ploidy cells show a reproducible glucose-starvation response shift
    across cell lines?

  Work
    - Generate publication-grade live/dead/glucose trajectory panels by cell
      line, ploidy, and starting glucose.
    - Show paired ploidy contrasts for growth, death, alive AUC, and
      glucose-linked yield.
    - Present all-line and SUM-159-fuse-excluded views.

  Pass criteria
    - At least one interpretable phenotype has consistent direction in >=4/5
      lines and stronger consistency in 4/4 after excluding SUM-159-fuse.
    - The effect is visible in raw or lightly smoothed trajectories, not only in
      derived summaries.

  Fail criteria
    - Effects are dominated by one line or vanish under simple replotting.

  Human-reviewable outputs
    - Figure 1: experiment schematic plus representative trajectories.
    - Figure 2: paired ploidy-effect heatmap or dot plot, with all-line and
      SUM-159-fuse-excluded panels.
    - Supplement: per-line trajectory grids.

WP2. Promote morphology / volume analysis

  Question
    Is ploidy-associated starvation behavior better explained by measured cell
    size or inferred volume than by ploidy label alone?

  Work
    - Use area quantiles from data/stan_ready_data_with_area.Rds and/or
      data/counts/batch_results_TEMP_MEASURE_SIZE.csv.
    - Model area as a function of ploidy, cell line, glucose, time, confluency /
      cell density, and growth phase.
    - Treat area as a proxy for volume, with volume_proxy = area^(3/2) as a
      sensitivity analysis.
    - Explicitly test whether SUM-159-fuse is the line with weak 2N/4N size
      separation.

  Candidate model
    log(area_q50 or area_q90) ~ ploidy * cellLine + log_live_density + glucose
      + time + growth_rate_proxy

  Pass criteria
    - 2N/4N area or inferred volume separation is clear in most lines.
    - SUM-159-fuse is quantitatively an outlier or near-null size-separation
      case.
    - Adding area/volume reduces or explains ploidy-associated starvation
      phenotype differences.

  Fail criteria
    - Area is too confounded with confluency/time to support interpretation.
    - SUM-159-fuse is not distinct after adjustment.

  Human-reviewable outputs
    - Figure 3: cell area/volume distributions by line and ploidy.
    - Figure 4: adjusted ploidy effect on area/volume with SUM-159-fuse
      highlighted.
    - Supplement: model coefficient table and residual diagnostics.

WP3. Nuclear size extension

  Question
    Does nuclear size track ploidy and/or explain why SUM-159-fuse behaves
    differently?

  Work
    - Resegment images if raw masks were not saved.
    - Use Cellpose or CellposeSAM for cell masks.
    - Within each cell mask, segment nuclear stain using thresholding and/or
      watershed.
    - Extract nuclear area, cell area, nuclear:cell area ratio, and nuclear
      stain intensity.
    - Start with a bounded pilot: two to three glucose conditions, early/mid/
      late timepoints, all cell lines and ploidies.

  Pass criteria
    - Nuclear area separates ploidy better than cell area, or nuclear:cell ratio
      identifies SUM-159-fuse as unusual.
    - Segmentation QC is good enough on a representative subset.

  Fail criteria
    - Nuclear segmentation is unreliable without manual correction.
    - Nuclear stain does not provide usable object-level signal.

  Human-reviewable outputs
    - Candidate Figure 5: cell area versus nuclear area scatter/density by line
      and ploidy.
    - QC panel: representative masks over raw images.
    - Table: segmentation success rate by line, ploidy, time, and glucose.

WP4. SUM-159-fuse exclusion analysis

  Question
    Is SUM-159-fuse masking a generalizable ploidy effect, given that it is both
    a protocol outlier and a volume outlier?

  Work
    - Rerun model-free summaries excluding SUM-159-fuse.
    - Rerun transfer/generalization benchmarks excluding SUM-159-fuse.
    - Recompute paired effect matrices and sign consistency.
    - Recheck the direction and cross-model agreement of the mechanistic
      ploidy effects on y1, v1, K1, and m.
    - Compare all-line and no-SUM-159-fuse results side by side.

  Pass criteria
    - Excluding SUM-159-fuse materially increases sign consistency, transfer
      performance, or effect-size coherence.
    - The reason for exclusion is biologically and experimentally motivated by
      protocol and morphology/volume, not only post hoc performance.
    - The y1/v1/K1/m direction conclusion remains conserved across tested
      models, moves further from zero, and shows stronger inter-model agreement
      after exclusion.

  Fail criteria
    - Exclusion does not improve coherence.
    - Another line becomes equally discordant.

  Human-reviewable outputs
    - Figure: all-lines versus no-SUM-159-fuse effect consistency.
    - Table: transfer/generalization metrics before and after exclusion.
    - Short written exception-analysis paragraph.

WP4b. No-SUM-159-fuse gpath generalization rerun

  Question
    Do mechanistic gpath transfer/generalization conclusions improve when
    SUM-159-fuse is excluded from the fitting and transfer benchmark itself,
    after the prior protocol, volume, and parameter-direction rationale has been
    established?

  Work
    - Build a no-SUM-159-fuse Stan-data object from data/stan_ready_data.Rds,
      with line, well, observation, group, and glucose-condition indices
      remapped rather than leaving empty line levels.
    - Rebuild the gpath optimisation dataset manifest for the no-SUM data:
      full-data fits plus low-to-high and high-to-low transfer/null datasets for
      the remaining four cell lines.
    - Rerun the gpath assessment model set through the maintained optimisation
      framework, using the existing Slurm optimisation pipeline rather than
      interactive jobs.
    - Start with a small smoke-test run and/or narrowed representative model
      subset before committing to the full 12-model, 500-start grid.
    - Compare no-SUM gpath transfer/null/oracle-style optimisation summaries
      against the existing all-line gpath generalization results.
    - Treat y1/v1/K1/m directionality and inter-model agreement as the key
      transferable mechanistic conclusion, separate from generic holdout fit.
    - Run NUTS only for selected no-SUM representative models after optimisation
      results justify the expense; do not run posterior sampling for every
      transfer split by default.

  Cost / reuse considerations
    - Existing all-line optimisation results are reusable as the baseline
      comparator, but not as final no-SUM fits because SUM-159-fuse contributed
      to the training target.
    - Existing transfer datasets for non-SUM holdouts are not sufficient for this
      question, because their training sets still include SUM-159-fuse.
    - Existing model code, assessment run IDs, optimisation wrappers, and Slurm
      submission scripts should be reused.
    - Existing NUTS draws are not reusable for no-SUM posterior claims because
      the posterior target changes.
    - Directly reusing optimisation draws as initial values requires care because
      excluding a line changes line, group, and well dimensions; prefer fresh
      multistart optimisation unless a validated parameter-mapping initializer
      is added.

  Pass criteria
    - No-SUM gpath transfer fits improve materially relative to null baselines
      and relative to the all-line benchmark on matched non-SUM holdouts.
    - The improvement is consistent across at least the representative model
      candidates, not only one flexible model.
    - Any no-SUM representative model chosen for interpretation has acceptable
      visual fit and, if sampled, acceptable NUTS diagnostics.

  Fail criteria
    - Excluding SUM-159-fuse does not improve gpath transfer/generalization
      performance after matching on the same four non-SUM holdout lines.
    - Improvement appears only in an over-flexible or visually poor model.
    - The no-SUM run is too unstable to support a mechanistic exclusion claim.

  Human-reviewable outputs
    - Table: all-line versus no-SUM gpath transfer/null/generalization metrics
      on matched non-SUM holdouts.
    - Figure: no-SUM versus all-line transfer gains by model, line, direction,
      and fit type.
    - Figure: visual fit overlays for selected no-SUM representative models.
    - Short decision note documenting which expensive jobs were run, which were
      intentionally skipped, and why.

WP5. Fix mechanistic representative model

  Question
    Can we show a mechanistic model whose fit is visually credible and whose
    claims are limited to what it supports?

  Work
    - Stop using 1R_1P_0W_C0_M1 as the representative model if its visual fit is
      poor.
    - Define representative model by a joint criterion: acceptable visual fit,
      reasonable AIC or held-out likelihood, stable parameter interpretation,
      no NUTS pathologies, and no obvious overfitting.
    - Consider best-AIC 2R_2P_1W_C0_M0 or a reduced task-1 model only if it is
      promoted into the canonical workflow.
    - Add fit-quality panels before biological interpretation.
    - Use existing canonical workflow overlay styles for fitting visualisations.

  Pass criteria
    - The representative model visibly captures live/dead/glucose dynamics
      across most line/ploidy/glucose regimes.
    - Model conclusions do not depend on a model with poor visual fit.
    - Selection-strategy simulations use a model set with acceptable fit/QC.

  Fail criteria
    - No current model has acceptable visual fit. In that case, mechanistic
      modeling should move to exploratory/supplementary status for the first
      draft.

  Human-reviewable outputs
    - Figure: model fit overlays for candidate representative models.
    - Figure: model ranking versus fit-quality tradeoff.
    - Decision table: selected model, rejected models, and rejection reasons.

WP6. Selection and competition claims

  Question
    Are the fitted dynamics strong enough to support selection claims?

  Work
    - Re-evaluate selection simulations after WP5 model choice.
    - Treat competition validation as a check, not primary evidence.
    - Report whether strategies select for high ploidy more robustly than
      against it under acceptable models.

  Pass criteria
    - The "for high ploidy" result remains robust across acceptable models and
      ideally after SUM-159-fuse exclusion.
    - Competition data match direction for at least the main qualitative claim.

  Fail criteria
    - Selection result depends on poor-fit models.
    - Competition validation contradicts simulations.

  Human-reviewable outputs
    - Figure: selection-strategy landscape for acceptable models.
    - Figure/table: competition validation observed versus predicted
      high-ploidy fraction or log ratio.

Recommended order

  1. WP4 first: fast, decisive, and directly tests the SUM-159-fuse concern.
  2. WP4b only if WP4/WP2 make SUM-159-fuse exclusion biologically credible or
     if mechanistic generalization becomes central to the draft.
  3. WP2 next: promotes morphology into the central story.
  4. WP5 before any strong ODE-based biological claims.
  5. WP3 as a bounded pilot because it may become a strong morphology mechanism
     figure but may be slower.
  6. WP6 only after deciding which mechanistic models are admissible.

Whole-paper pass/fail gate

  After excluding or mechanistically explaining SUM-159-fuse, do ploidy-associated
  starvation effects align with area/volume differences strongly enough to
  support a coherent biological mechanism? If yes, the article has a clean
  volume-mediated ploidy-cost spine. If no, the first draft should become a more
  descriptive paper about heterogeneous consequences of genome doubling under
  nutrient limitation.
