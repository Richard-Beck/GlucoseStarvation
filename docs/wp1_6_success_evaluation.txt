WP1-6 goal and success evaluation

Date: 2026-05-12

Source context

  This note summarizes the completed publication work packages defined in
  docs/publication_work_packages.txt and evaluated from the corresponding
  report exports under data/report_exports/wp*/ and figures under figures/wp*/.

Overall goal

  The work packages were designed to test the first-draft thesis that ploidy
  changes glucose-starvation physiology, probably through cell-size or
  resource-demand-linked effects, but that the effect is not universal across
  all engineered comparisons. The whole-paper gate was whether SUM-159-fuse
  could be excluded or mechanistically explained, and whether the remaining
  ploidy-associated starvation effects align with area/volume differences well
  enough to support a coherent volume-mediated ploidy-cost story.

Overall assessment

  Implementation success is high: the planned figures, audit tables, decision
  notes, and reruns exist for the WP1-6 evidence stack.

  Scientific success is partial. The evidence supports a descriptive and
  qualified mechanism: ploidy-associated starvation phenotypes are real but
  heterogeneous; morphology/volume explains an important part; SUM-159-fuse is
  a justified biological/protocol exception candidate. The evidence does not
  fully support a strong universal volume-mediated ploidy-cost mechanism with
  robust mechanistic transfer and competition validation.

Package-level assessment

  WP1. Core starvation phenotype
    Passed, but narrowly. One interpretable feature, Alive AUC glucose response,
    met the sign-consistency gate: 4/5 lines all-line and 4/4 after excluding
    SUM-159-fuse. Other growth, death, and yield summaries were weaker or mixed.

  WP2. Morphology / volume analysis
    Strong success. Size or inferred-volume separation is clear in most lines,
    and SUM-159-fuse is a genuine outlier: adjusted q50 log2 ratio about -0.35,
    latent-volume log2 ratio about -0.55, and z-scores around -2.6 and -2.3
    versus non-SUM lines. Area explains part of the phenotype, not all of it;
    the best phenotype-area summary gives roughly 28% RMSE reduction and R2
    around 0.48.

  WP3. Nuclear size extension
    Successful as a bounded pilot and QC extension. The completed run has 90/90
    images with ok status and about 99.8% alive-cell nuclear success. Nuclear
    area tracks high ploidy in several lines, but not MDA-MB-231 or
    SUM-159-fuse, so it reinforces heterogeneity rather than proving a universal
    nuclear-size mechanism.

  WP4. SUM-159-fuse exclusion analysis
    Biologically motivated, but not performance-decisive. Excluding
    SUM-159-fuse improves the main WP1 passing feature and some consistency
    summaries, but the average model-free consistency shift is small and mixed.
    Transfer performance does not improve on matched non-SUM holdouts. The
    strongest exclusion rationale remains protocol plus morphology/volume plus
    directional mechanistic coherence, not benchmark performance alone.

  WP4b. No-SUM-159-fuse gpath generalization rerun
    Computationally complete, scientifically mixed to negative for transfer.
    The no-SUM optimization grid completed, but mean no-SUM minus all-line
    transfer/null holdout gain is negative, with only about half of matched
    cases improving. This does not support a strong claim that excluding
    SUM-159-fuse materially improves gpath generalization.

  WP5. Mechanistic representative model
    Successful as a model-governance fix. The legacy 1R_1P_0W_C0_M1 model is no
    longer treated as the representative because of visual-fit concerns.
    2R_2P_0W_C0_M1 is the provisional representative with acceptable visual
    review and passing NUTS QC. Strong claims should remain focused on fitted
    alive/glucose dynamics because the current overlay path does not provide a
    separate fitted dead-cell trajectory.

  WP6. Selection and competition claims
    Simulation support exists, but empirical validation is weak for the primary
    model. Acceptable models generally predict that strategies for high ploidy
    are stronger than strategies against high ploidy, including the no-SUM
    global check. Competition validation is not reassuring for the primary
    model and should remain a qualitative check rather than primary evidence.

Practical manuscript implication

  The most defensible first-draft spine is: ploidy-associated
  glucose-starvation phenotypes are reproducible for selected summaries but
  heterogeneous across lines; morphology/volume is a major explanatory axis;
  SUM-159-fuse is a biologically and experimentally justified exception; ODE
  and selection analyses should be framed as qualified model-supported
  hypotheses rather than central proof.
