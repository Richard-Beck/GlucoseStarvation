## Figure 3. Mechanistic model family and fit diagnostics
(a) Schematic of the ordinary differential equation model family. Measured extracellular glucose is resource R1; models may additionally include a fitted hidden resource R2, allocation to energy and precursor/biomass production, maintenance cost, and optional waste effects. Four resource configurations--one resource (1R), two resources feeding one pathway [2R(a), shown as “2R additive”], two resources with locked allocation [2R(l)], and two resources with flexible allocation [2R(f)]--are crossed with three waste modes--none, additive waste-associated death [W(a)], and multiplicative waste inhibition [W(m)]--to give 12 assessed structures. The schematic summarizes the model family; icon counts are illustrative rather than quantitative.

(b) Five-line global comparison, including SUM-159-fuse, of the 12 structures using the operational optimized-target score labeled deviance (lower is better) and the operational mechanistic complexity count k. The score is -2 times the best optimized Stan target; because that target includes priors and the Jacobian adjustment, the score is not a likelihood deviance. The count k comprises active mechanistic structural slots across five line effects and one shared ploidy effect and is not the total number of fitted Stan parameters. Blue-outlined points are the five non-dominated models under these two operational axes, and gray points are the other seven assessed structures. This Pareto set is an operational filter rather than an inferential model probability or evidence that a retained structure is true or uniquely preferred. Callouts identify the retained 1R, 1R,W(m), 2R(a), 2R(f), and 2R(f),W(m) models. No uncertainty intervals are shown.

(c) Condition-level observation-likelihood loss for the five retained models in the primary four-line analysis excluding SUM-159-fuse. Rows are cell-line/ploidy conditions, columns are starting-glucose G0 values in mM, and facets are model structures. For each line, ploidy state, and G0, tile color is the nonnegative observation-likelihood loss per well relative to the best of the five displayed models for that condition; zero denotes the best displayed model and darker red denotes greater loss. This locally computed loss is distinct from the panel-b optimized-target score and is not a likelihood deviance, although the color bar is labeled “Deviance loss per well.” Fill is capped at the 95th percentile of displayed losses, so saturated tiles can exceed the upper color-bar range. SUM-159-fuse is omitted, whereas SUM-159-chem is retained. R2 and waste states are fitted, model-implied quantities rather than direct measurements of an environmental resource or waste product.

## Figure 4. Ploidy displacement within apparent-parameter geometry
Posterior geometry for the five Pareto-selected models in the primary four-line analysis excluding SUM-159-fuse. The four fitted apparent parameters are per-cell yield (y1), apparent glucose uptake (v1), glucose response scale (K1), and maintenance/death (m). Posterior-median parameter values were log2 transformed and each parameter was centered and scaled once across all five models, four lines, and two ploidy states. These are reconstructed apparent model parameters, not direct biochemical measurements.

(a) Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) of Euclidean distances among the globally standardized four-parameter points. Facets show model structures. Colored arrows connect the low-ploidy state to the high-ploidy state for each line; arrowheads mark high ploidy. Black points are model centroids across line and ploidy points. Ellipses centered at the high-ploidy endpoints are 80% posterior displacement ellipses propagated through the fixed ordination. They quantify displacement uncertainty conditional on the selected model, data, scaling, and projection; they do not include uncertainty in absolute low-ploidy position, model selection, model weights, or between-model choice. PCoA axis labels give the percentage of positive-eigenvalue variation represented by each axis. Blue, orange, green, and pink denote MCF10A, MDA-MB-231, SNU668, and SUM-159-chem, respectively.

(b) Correlations of the four standardized apparent parameters with the two PCoA axes. Arrows provide orientation aids for the ordination and are not regression coefficients or uncertainty estimates; the gray circle is a unit reference.

(c) Exact unprojected pairwise distances among line profiles. Each line profile concatenates low- and high-ploidy values of the four standardized parameters into eight dimensions. Cells show the median root-mean-square (RMS) distance across the five models; darker teal denotes greater dimensionless distance.

(d) Exact unprojected within-line ploidy distance for each model and line, calculated as the four-dimensional RMS of the standardized high-minus-low parameter components. Darker purple denotes greater dimensionless distance.

(e) Exact unprojected pairwise distances among model centroids. Each centroid is the mean standardized four-parameter profile across all line and ploidy points within a model; cells show four-dimensional RMS distance, with darker blue denoting greater distance.

(f) Signed standardized high-minus-low contributions for y1, v1, K1, and m by model and line. Positive values (red) indicate a higher standardized parameter value in high ploidy, negative values (blue) indicate a lower value, and near-zero values are white. These components are not normalized shares and are not constrained to sum to one. Panels c-f show numerical summaries without uncertainty intervals or significance tests.

## Figure S7. Reciprocal ploidy-transfer stress-test diagnostics
(a) Schematic of the reciprocal ploidy-transfer test. Models are trained on other-line data and one ploidy state from a target line, then evaluated on the opposite-ploidy state held out from that line. The null fit uses the same train/holdout split but removes the ploidy effect, the transfer fit predicts the held-out ploidy, and the box labeled “Oracle” is a target-inclusive full-data fitted reference used for trajectory comparison; it is not an independent prospective validation target or a split-specific refit. The transfer score is the summed held-out log likelihood for the transfer fit minus that for the matched null fit; positive values favor transfer and negative values favor null. (b) Four-line, SUM-159-fuse-excluded transfer-score heatmaps for 12 model structures and both directions. High-to-low uses the high-ploidy state for fitting and holds out low ploidy; low-to-high does the reverse. Red and blue denote positive and negative scores, respectively. Fill is clipped at ±1,500, whereas labels report the unclipped scores; scores are natural-log-scale sums over the held-out observations and are not normalized per observation. Outlined cells identify Case C, MCF10A low-to-high under 2R(f),W(m), and Case D, SNU668 high-to-low under 2R(f). The 96 model-line-direction scores are descriptive fixed-case comparisons and have no inferential uncertainty intervals. (c) Held-out trajectory overlays for Case C across G0 = 0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 5, and 25 mM: MCF10A low-to-high transfer to ploidy 4 under 2R(f),W(m), with a transfer-minus-null score of +494. (d) Corresponding overlays for Case D: SNU668 high-to-low transfer to ploidy 2.6 under 2R(f), with a score of -802. In panels c and d, black points are observed alive-cell counts N or calibrated extracellular glucose R1; gray short-dashed, blue solid, and red dashed curves show null, transfer, and full-data reference fits, respectively. Y-axis scales vary among G0 rows. Lines are trajectories from the selected optimization fits; no uncertainty bands are shown, and the full-data reference is not an independent validation target.
