## Results Text

We next asked whether the fitted effect of ploidy was large and mechanistically consistent relative to variation among cell lines and model structures. To compare these sources of variation on a common scale, we represented posterior-median values of four apparent parameters—yield (`y1`), uptake (`v1`), glucose response (`K1`), and maintenance/death (`m`)—after global standardization across five Pareto-front model structures, four cell lines, and two ploidy states. In the resulting parameter space, low-to-high-ploidy displacements were aligned within each model but occupied different backgrounds across lines and model structures (Figure 4a,b). Median exact distances between eight-dimensional line profiles ranged from 0.90 for SNU668 versus SUM-159-chem to 1.29 for MCF10A versus SNU668 (Figure 4c). By comparison, exact within-model ploidy distances ranged from 0.24 to 1.00 and were consistently smaller for MDA-MB-231 (0.24–0.33) than for the other three lines (0.71–1.00; Figure 4d). Model-centroid distances spanned a much broader relative range, from 0.15 for the closely related 1R pair to 1.38 between 2R(a) and 2R(f),W(m) (Figure 4e). Thus, line background generally exceeded the within-model ploidy displacement, whereas model structure altered the fitted background by amounts ranging from minor to the largest displayed centroid separation.

The parameter decomposition identified one sign pattern that was stable across this hierarchy. In every one of the 20 model-by-line comparisons, `v1` and `K1` were higher in the high-ploidy state, whereas `y1` and `m` were lower (Figure 4f). These posterior fits therefore identify higher apparent glucose uptake and a higher glucose-response parameter, lower apparent per-cell yield, and lower maintenance/death pressure as recurrent high-ploidy changes in the primary four-line analysis. Lower fitted `m` is compatible with greater robustness to starvation, but it does not distinguish maintenance from death or directly measure survival. Model structure still altered the magnitude of these shifts: the negative `y1` contrast approached zero in the flexible 2R models, whereas the largest negative `m` contrasts occurred in those same models (Figure 4f). Model structure therefore changed the mechanistic allocation and strength of the ploidy effect without changing its fitted parameter directions.

The 80% displacement ellipses quantify posterior uncertainty in the projected low-to-high-ploidy shift conditional on each model and dataset, rather than uncertainty due to model choice. Separation among model centroids and model-dependent shift magnitudes capture structural variation outside those ellipses (Figure 4a,e,f). All ten displayed four-line primary and five-line sensitivity fits had no flagged Rhat, bulk-ESS, or tail-ESS diagnostics. Twenty-four of the 25 single-line fits were likewise unflagged; only the SNU668 single-line 2R(f),W(m) fit had minor bulk- and tail-ESS flags (Figure S10c). The five-line sensitivity estimates were generally attenuated toward zero relative to the four-line primary fits while retaining negative `y1` and `m` and positive `v1` and `K1` point summaries (Figure S10b). These checks show broad directional agreement across dataset scopes while preserving model structure as an important boundary on mechanistic interpretation.

Because `y1` and `v1` are expressed per cell, we next placed these effects in cell-size context. Adding nuclear-area or latent-volume-proxy ratios converted the uniformly negative per-cell yield summaries into a heterogeneous pattern: size-filled yield was positive for MCF10A and SUM-159-chem, negative for MDA-MB-231, and near zero for SNU668. Size-filled uptake remained positive for MCF10A, SNU668, and SUM-159-chem but shifted slightly below zero for MDA-MB-231 (Figure S10a). The hypothesis that high ploidy increases volumetric yield therefore receives only partial, proxy-based support. Moreover, the morphology covariates did not support a simple proportional mapping from ploidy to cell size. Although the displayed elevated-member ploidy covariate was positive in all five cell-line pairs, cell-area values ranged from −0.39 to 1.02 and nuclear-area values from 0.06 to 0.96, including essentially unchanged cell area in MDA-MB-231 and decreased cell area in SUM-159-fuse (Figure S13a). In exploratory all-five-line fits, substituting nuclear area for ploidy lowered likelihood AIC by 136–240 points across all five model structures, whereas substituting cell area raised it by 76–243 points; condition-level nuclear-area log-likelihood changes nevertheless varied in sign across cell lines, starting glucose concentrations, and models (Figure S13b,c). Ploidy- and nuclear-area-based fits yielded the same broad parameter directions—higher `v1` and `K1`, lower `y1` and `m`—but the realized nuclear-area effects varied more among lines and were nearly absent in SUM-159-fuse (Figure S14a,b). Taken together, the posterior evidence identifies higher apparent uptake and response together with lower fitted per-cell yield and maintenance/death as consistent high-ploidy shifts, but their magnitude and mechanistic attribution remain constrained by model structure and the failure of cell size to scale simply with ploidy.

## Revision Notes

1. The current F4 parameter decomposition has uniform directions in all 20 model-by-line comparisons: `y1` and `m` are negative, whereas `v1` and `K1` are positive. The v02 statements that `y1` changed sign across models and that only `v1`/`m` were directionally stable were not retained.
2. C1 and C2 are stated as model-derived apparent uptake and per-cell yield changes rather than direct biochemical measurements. C3 is linked locally to lower fitted maintenance/death as compatible with, but not a direct assay of, starvation robustness.
3. C12 remains a working hypothesis because the volume-proxy yield evidence is positive in two lines, negative in one, and near zero in one; it is not a direct volumetric-yield measurement.
4. Current S13 and S14 replace the obsolete v02 leave-one-line and prediction-transfer interpretations. Their morphology-covariate comparisons are exploratory all-five-line fits without displayed posterior or model-selection uncertainty.
5. The strong reconciled counterevidence to simple C7 proportionality is reflected by explicitly rejecting a fixed ploidy-to-size mapping. Current S13a shows heterogeneous cell- and nuclear-area covariates, including near-zero and reversed cell-area contrasts despite positive ploidy contrasts.
6. Figure 4 ellipses are used only as model-conditional posterior uncertainty summaries. The unsupported v02 numerical ellipse-to-arrow ratio was removed, and no ordering of posterior versus model-choice uncertainty is claimed.
